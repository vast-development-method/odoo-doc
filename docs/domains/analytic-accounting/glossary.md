# Analytic Accounting — Glossary

Every term this folder uses, defined in full. Terms are listed alphabetically. Where a term has a
reproduced identifier — a transport name, a storage name or a stored selection value — the
identifier is given in code font beside it; the definition never depends on recognising the
identifier.

---

**All-accounts count** (`all_account_count`) — The number of analytic accounts in a plan **and in
every descendant plan**, obtained by matching the descendants on the materialised path. It is the
figure the *Analytic Accounts* button of a plan shows, and it decides whether the plan is offered in
a distribution editor at all: a plan whose whole sub-tree is empty is never offered.

**Analytic account** (`account.analytic.account`) — One value on one axis of analysis: a project, a
department, a cost centre, a campaign, a region, a vehicle, a customer contract. It belongs to
exactly one plan and therefore to exactly one root plan, carries an optional reference and an
optional customer, may be restricted to one company or shared, and may be archived. It is what a
distribution attributes an amount to and what an analytic line is tagged with.

**Analytic accounting** — The second, parallel bookkeeping this domain specifies. Financial
accounting classifies an amount by its nature; analytic accounting classifies the same amount by
its purpose. The two books share their source events and are structurally independent: an analytic
line does not balance, has no debit and credit columns and is not part of the double-entry
invariant.

**Analytic distribution** (`analytic_distribution`) — A structured document attached to a record
that carries an amount, mapping combinations of analytic accounts to percentages. It states how the
record's amount is to be attributed. It is not itself an amount: it becomes amounts only when
analytic lines are generated from it.

**Analytic Distribution Model** (`account.analytic.distribution.model`) — A standing instruction
that proposes a distribution to a document line whose partner, partner category, product, product
category, financial account prefix and company satisfy its conditions. Several models may be
combined, each filling the root plans that no higher-priority model and no related source document
has already filled.

**Analytic item** — The name the interface shows for an Analytic Line. The two terms mean the same
record.

**Analytic Line** (`account.analytic.line`) — One dated, signed amount, with an optional quantity
and unit, attributed to one analytic account per axis. A positive amount is revenue, a negative
amount is cost. Most analytic lines are generated by the posting of a journal item; some are created
directly by another domain — a timesheet line is an analytic line — and some are entered by hand.

**Analytic Mixin** (`analytic.mixin`) — The contract a record implements to carry a distribution. It
has no table of its own and contributes the distribution field, its normalisation, its search, its
grouping, its validation and its merge behaviour to every entity that implements it.

**Analytic Plan** (`account.analytic.plan`) — One axis of analysis, grouping analytic accounts.
Plans form a tree; the topmost ancestor of a plan is its root plan. Only root plans own a stored
column, have an applicability and are validated.

**Analytic Plan Applicability** (`account.analytic.applicability`) — See *applicability rule*.

**Analytic Plan Fields Mixin** (`analytic.plan.fields.mixin`) — The contract a record implements to
carry **one analytic account per root plan** instead of a distribution. A record implementing it
holds at most one account per axis, whatever the depth of the sub-plan that account belongs to.

**Applicability** — The status of a plan in a given situation: `optional`, an account may be
attributed; `mandatory`, the plan's percentages must total exactly one hundred before the document
can be confirmed or posted from the interface; `unavailable`, the plan is not offered at all.

**Applicability rule** — A record attached to a root plan that overrides that plan's default
applicability for one business domain, optionally narrowed by a financial account prefix, a product
category and a company. Rules compete by a score, and the highest-scoring rule that beats the
baseline wins.

**Archived account** — An analytic account whose active flag is false. It is hidden from the default
lists and from the value lists of the distribution editor; existing distributions and existing
analytic lines keep referring to it; and no journal entry whose distribution names it can be posted.

**Back-computation** — The rebuilding of a journal item's distribution **from** its analytic lines,
run whenever an analytic line is created, edited or deleted by hand. It is the reverse direction of
the generation, and it is what keeps the two books in step when a reader edits the analytic side.

**Base plan** — The one root plan designated by the system parameter `analytic.project_plan`. It is
the only plan whose stored column has a fixed name, `account_id`, instead of a name derived from its
identifier; it may never be given a parent; and it always comes first wherever the root plans are
enumerated. The plan shipped as reference data for this role is named *Project*.

**Baseline** — The score of zero point five that an applicability rule must beat **strictly** to
override a plan's default applicability. A rule whose only distinguishing criterion is the company
scores exactly zero point five and therefore never wins on its own.

**Business domain** (`business_domain`) — The kind of document an applicability rule applies to, and
the value the caller passes when asking for the applicability of a plan: Miscellaneous, Invoice,
Vendor Bill, Expense, Timesheet, Purchase Order, Sale Order, Manufacturing Order or Stock Picking.
The list of values depends on which capabilities are installed.

**Category** (`category`) — The classification of an analytic line by the kind of document it came
from: `other`, `invoice`, `vendor_bill`, `manufacturing_order` or `picking_entry`. It is used by the
profitability classification and by the project profitability sections, which exclude the last two.

**Closing-line rule** — The rule that gives the distribution entry which brings a plan's cumulative
percentage to **exactly** one hundred the exact amount not yet distributed for that plan, instead of
its own proportional share. It is what makes the analytic lines of a fully distributed plan add up
exactly to the negated balance of the journal item.

**Colour index** (`color`) — A whole number from one to eleven that tints a plan's chip in the
distribution editor and the card of its accounts. A plan takes a pseudo-random one at creation; an
account inherits its plan's.

**Combination** — One key of a distribution: the identifiers of one or more analytic accounts,
written as decimal numbers separated by commas, naming at most one account per root plan. A
combination expresses a cross-axis attribution — "sixty percent to Research and Development of the
Departments axis together with Project Alpha of the Project axis" — and produces **one** analytic
line carrying all of its accounts.

**Company-dependent value** — A field that stores one value per company for the same record, reading
back the value of the company the reader is acting for. The default applicability of a plan is the
only such field in this domain.

**Compatibility finding** — A behaviour recorded in this folder as observed, marked as such, because
it looks like a defect; the note says what a corrected behaviour would be, so that a rebuild chooses
knowingly. This domain records four: the name resolution of a database search on a distribution; the
concurrent closing of two plans by one distribution entry; the plan total used by the redistribution
of a valuation document; and the default grouping requested by the two analytic item screens.

**Complete name** (`complete_name`) — The stored, recursive name of a plan: the parent's complete
name, a space, a solidus, a space and the plan's own name. It is the plan's display name.

**Cross product** — The step of the merge that pairs every key of the non-changing side with every
key of the changing side, producing one combined key per pair with a value proportional to both.

**Default applicability** (`default_applicability`) — The applicability a root plan takes when no
applicability rule beats the baseline. It is required on the form of a root plan, hidden on the form
of a sub-plan, and stored per company.

**Depth** — The distance of a plan from the root of its hierarchy: zero for a root plan, one for its
direct children, two for their children, and one more for each further level. It is computed as the
number of solidus characters in the materialised path minus one, and one grouping column exists per
depth that actually contains a plan.

**Discount allocation account** — The account to which a company books the discounts it grants,
instead of reducing its income accounts. The journal item booked on it receives a distribution
weighted by the distributions of the product lines whose discounts it allocates.

**Distribution editor** — The single component through which a distribution is entered anywhere in
the system: a grid with one column per relevant plan, a percentage column and, when the host field
names an amount, a monetary column. Its closed form is a row of one tag per plan.

**Elevated rights** — A read performed without the reader's own access restrictions. Four reads of
this domain are elevated and must stay so in a rebuild: the resolution of the root plans; the
company consistency count when an account's company changes; the name search on an account's
customer; and the archived-account check at posting.

**Financial account** (`general_account_id`) — The account of the financial chart an analytic line
was booked on. It is copied from the journal item and must stay equal to that item's account for as
long as the line points at it. It drives the profitability classification.

**Forced applicability** — An applicability imposed by the caller instead of computed from the
rules. It makes every root plan with at least one account visible with that applicability and stops
every rule from being scored. The distribution editor of a distribution model uses it.

**Forced plan** — A plan the applicability rules would hide, shown in the distribution editor
anyway because the distribution already names one of its accounts. It is always shown as `optional`,
so that the percentage already recorded stays visible and editable.

**Generated column** — The stored analytic account column that a **non-base** root plan owns on
every plan-bearing entity, named `x_plan`, the plan's identifier and `_id`. It is created when the
plan becomes a root and deleted when the plan is deleted or demoted.

**Gross Margin** — The label under which an analytic account's balance is presented, on the form's
button and on the screen that button opens: the revenue attributed to the account minus the cost
attributed to it.

**Grouping column** — A derived, read-only, unstored column that exposes the sub-plan of a given
depth to which a plan-bearing record's account belongs, so that a report can be grouped by sub-plan
level. Its name is the root plan's column name, an underscore and the depth, prefixed when the
result would otherwise begin with `account_id`.

**Invalid analytics flag** (`has_invalid_analytics`) — A derived flag on a journal item marking a
product line whose distribution fails the mandatory plan validation, excluding lines booked on a
receivable, payable, cash or credit card account. It highlights the line before posting and does not
by itself prevent posting.

**Journal item** — One line of a journal entry, carrying a debit or a credit on one financial
account. It is the main carrier of a distribution and the source from which most analytic lines are
generated. It belongs to [../general-ledger/](../general-ledger/README.md).

**Leftover** — The part of the merge that the cross product cannot express: when one side of the
merge carries a larger total than the other, the surplus is kept as entries naming only that side's
accounts, so that each side's total survives the merge.

**Magic column** (`auto_account_id`) — The helper column of a plan-bearing record that stands for
**all** the plan columns at once. Reading it returns the account of the plan named in the reader's
context; writing it stores the account in the column of that account's own root plan; searching it
expands into a disjunction over every root plan's column. It is what lets one default value, one
search condition or one collection of lines work for every axis.

**Mandatory plan** — A plan whose computed applicability is `mandatory` in the situation at hand.
The percentages attributed to accounts of that root plan must total exactly one hundred, compared
after rounding both operands to the percentage precision, before the document can be confirmed or
posted from the interface.

**Materialised path** (`parent_path`) — The slash-separated chain of ancestor identifiers of a plan,
ending with the plan's own identifier and a trailing solidus. Descendant tests are prefix tests on
it, and the depth is derived from it.

**Partial-update marker** (`__update__`) — The reserved key of a distribution **being written**,
whose value is the list of stored column names of the root plans the incoming document intends to
replace. It triggers the merge instead of a replacement, and it never survives in a stored
distribution.

**Partial-update merge** — The algorithm that replaces the part of a distribution concerning a
chosen set of root plans while keeping the rest, by multiplying the two sides key by key and
balancing their totals with a ratio and a leftover.

**Percentage precision** — The number of decimal places, two, of the decimal precision record named
`Percentage Analytic`. Every percentage is rounded to it on write, and every comparison of a plan
total against one hundred is made at it. The distribution editor works with two additional digits
and rounds back.

**Plan-bearing entity** — An entity that implements the Analytic Plan Fields Mixin and therefore
holds one analytic account column per root plan. Analytic Line is the one this domain owns.

**Plan column** — The stored analytic account column of one root plan on a plan-bearing entity:
`account_id` for the base plan and a generated column for every other root plan.

**Profitability classification** (`analytic_profitability`) — The derived classification of an
analytic line into `revenue`, `loss` or `uncategorized`, read first from the type of its financial
account and, failing that, from its category and the sign of its amount.

**Project profitability sections** — The three sections a project's profitability panel receives
from this domain's project accounting capability: the vendor bills that mention the project's
analytic account but come from no purchase order; the other revenues; and the other costs, the last
two taken from the analytic lines of that account that have no journal item.

**Ratio** — The factor the merge applies to the cross product so that the side with the smaller
total is scaled up to meet the larger one. It is one when the two totals are equal, and it is reset
to one when the non-changing side is the larger.

**Redistribution** — The in-place adjustment of the analytic lines already attached to a valuation
document when that document recomputes its cost. Unlike the regeneration that follows a distribution
change on a posted journal item, it preserves the identifiers of the surviving lines.

**Reference** (`code`) — The short code of an analytic account, shown in square brackets before the
name in the display name and matched by a name search.

**Relevant plans** — The ordered list of plans a distribution editor must show for one document
line, each with its applicability, computed from the applicability rules, the account counts and the
accounts already named in the line's distribution.

**Root plan** (`root_plan_id`, `root_id`) — A plan with no parent, and, of any plan, the topmost
ancestor. Only a root plan owns a stored column, has an applicability and is totalled when a
distribution is validated; a document line holds at most one analytic account per root plan.

**Rounding-error cancellation** — The second pass that runs after the analytic lines of one journal
item have been rounded: the accumulated drift is pushed back onto the candidates one by one,
starting with the **first**, in steps of at least one rounding unit, until it is zero.

**Score** — The number an applicability rule earns in a situation: zero point five for a matching
company, one for each of the matching business domain, the matching account prefix and the matching
product category, and minus one when any of those three criteria is present on the rule and does not
match. The highest score strictly above the baseline wins.

**Situation** — The set of arguments passed when the applicability of a plan is asked for: the
business domain, the company, the product, the financial account, a forced applicability and the
accounts already present. Every member is optional, and a member that is not supplied matches only
the rules that leave the corresponding criterion empty.

**Slice** — One analytic line's share of a journal item's amount, computed from one entry of the
distribution.

**Split** — The operation that replaces one analytic line by several in proportion to a
distribution written on it: the first set of values is written onto the line itself and each
remaining set creates a copy.

**Sub-plan** — A plan with a parent. It owns no stored column — its accounts live in the column of
its root plan — it has no applicability of its own, it is never offered in a distribution editor,
and its level is exposed through a grouping column.

**Synchronisation guard** — The marker a caller raises so that writing analytic lines does not
rewrite the distribution they came from, and writing a distribution does not delete and regenerate
the analytic lines. Every operation in which the system itself writes analytic lines runs with the
guard raised, which is what stops the two directions from looping.

**Validation flag** — The signal a caller sends to say that mandatory plans must be enforced. Only
the interactive posting and confirmation actions set it; programmatic posting, the automatic posting
job and the exchange difference entry do not, and therefore never block on a mandatory plan.

**Valuation document** — An inventory or manufacturing record that recomputes its own cost and keeps
its analytic lines up to date through the redistribution rather than through a journal item's
distribution.

**Zero test** — The check that an amount rounds to zero at a given step. A candidate analytic line
whose amount passes the zero test at the **company** currency's rounding step is not created at all
and contributes nothing to the rounding pass.
